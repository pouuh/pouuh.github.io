About

I am a Global China Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the Boston University Global Development Policy Center, with a co-appointment at the Center for Remote Sensing, working with Prof. Mark A. Friedl. I received my Ph.D. in Geography and Environmental Studies from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where my dissertation developed phenology-guided deep learning frameworks for high-accuracy soybean yield prediction across the Americas. Prior to joining BU, I was a Frontera Computational Science Fellow at the Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC).

My research combines satellite remote sensing, geospatial artificial intelligence, and high-performance computing to study agricultural systems, land-use change, and the environmental footprints of global development finance.

Research

Development Finance & the Environment

How do overseas investment and the energy transition reshape landscapes in the Global South? I combine development-finance databases with satellite-derived land change and spatial analysis to trace the environmental footprints of infrastructure and energy projects.

  • Land use & land cover change
  • Causal inference
  • Development finance
  • Energy transition

2025–2026 · Ongoing · Global China Program, BU GDP Center

Mapping the Environmental Impacts of Chinese Overseas Investments

This project examines how Chinese overseas investment shapes land use and land cover change across the Global South. Integrating overseas infrastructure finance databases with NASA MEaSUREs 30 m long-term LULCC records, it moves from spatial association to project-timed causal inference (difference-in-differences) and financier-specific attribution, revealing land-change patterns and their implications for carbon, biodiversity, and water resources.

2025– · Ongoing · BU GDP Center

Spatial Analysis of Mining and Energy Infrastructure

A global geospatial analysis of how mining activities and energy infrastructure are distributed and co-located. Integrating open project-level energy databases, satellite-derived mining footprints, and high-resolution imagery, this work examines the spatial relationships between resource extraction and the expansion of power generation.

Data: GEM Global Solar Power Tracker (CC BY 4.0); Maus et al. 2022 (CC BY 4.0); Owen et al. 2022 (underlying data: S&P Global). Basemap © Esri, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics.

GeoAI for Agriculture

From individual fields to entire continents, I develop geospatial AI and scalable computing methods that turn satellite observations into actionable information on crop growth, yield, and management.

  • Deep learning
  • Crop phenology
  • Spatial statistics
  • High-performance computing

2024–2025 · Frontera Computational Science Fellowship, TACC

Scalable Computational Framework for Near-Real-Time Soybean Yield Forecasting

A scalable high-performance computing framework for near-real-time soybean yield forecasting. A phenology-matching model identifies growth stages from satellite time series and couples them with crop growth modeling; space-time decomposition and core-level parallelization on the Frontera supercomputer enable continental-scale forecasting across the United States, Brazil, and Argentina, supporting food security early warning, commodity markets, and crop insurance.

2023 · Paper + Code

PB-CNN: Phenology-Guided Bayesian CNN for Soybean Yield Estimation

A phenology-guided Bayesian CNN that aligns satellite observations with crop growth stages to estimate U.S. county-level soybean yield with quantified uncertainty.

Paper Code

2025 · Paper + Code

QP-GWR: Site-Specific Economically Optimal Input Rates

A quadratic-plateau geographically weighted regression that captures spatially varying, nonlinear yield responses to estimate site-specific economically optimal nitrogen rates from on-farm experiments.

Paper Code

Reproduced from Zhang et al. (2025), Computers and Electronics in Agriculture.
